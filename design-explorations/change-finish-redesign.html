Change finish: redesign options

Static mocks for the prototype's Change finish modal, using the Primary Bath, Polished Nickel → Burnished Brass case (7 lines that can change; one has no brass version, one is a rough-in, one a tile). Same QOM tokens as the prototype. Mock data only; nothing is wired up.

Audit of the current modal

Step 1 · Select

  1. Two left edges. The subline is indented under the title, while the fields and list start at the dialog edge, so the header doesn't read as one block.
  2. The main decision isn't emphasised. Current finish and New finish are equal weight; the eye lands on "All finishes" first.
  3. Three levels of checkboxes (Select all, room, line) for what's usually "everything in this room".
  4. Dense rows. Five facts on one grey line (item, finish, qty, price, delivery) at 12px; price, the one number SCs compare, isn't aligned.
  5. Lines that can't change sit between ones that can, each taking a full row with a red reason, so the list reads as a list of problems.
  6. Footer is a lone grey button whose label is an instruction ("Choose a finish"); no summary of what Next will do.

Step 2 · Confirm

  1. The title is a two-line sentence carrying every key fact; it's the hardest thing on the screen to scan.
  2. "Will change" repeats a five-row label/value table per line, mostly "unchanged", and repeats the finish change that's identical on every line.
  3. No net impact up front: the quote total sits at the bottom and "+$1,176" is left for the SC to work out.
  4. Needs attention and the payment warning are in two places (top and under the buttons), styled differently.
  5. Heavy chrome: icon circle, eyebrow, black room band, two accordions and a grey total box stack up before any content.
  6. Step progress is a grey phrase ("Step 2 of 2 · Nothing has changed yet") rather than something visible.

A · Refined

Recommended

Keeps today's two steps and controls, fixes hierarchy. A step bar replaces the grey step phrase. From → To reads as one sentence with an arrow, and New finish is the heavier field. Rows put price in its own right-aligned column, and lines that can't change fold into one group at the bottom. Confirm leads with three numbers (lines, total change, needs attention), then a compact table that shows only what changes.

B · Table-first

Treats it as a bulk edit, like the quote grid SCs already use. A toolbar holds From, To and the room scope. Step 1 is a sortable table with a checkbox column and a "Now" price, and step 2 is the same table with New item, Δ price and Delivery columns plus a running total in the footer. It's the densest and fastest for big quotes, but it reads more like a spreadsheet than a decision.

C · Summary card

Two columns: the work on the left and a live summary card on the right (From → To swatches, lines, total change, attention count) that stays put while the list scrolls. Finishes are chips with coverage, so the choice is visible without opening a dropdown. Confirm keeps the card and turns the list into "check these first" and "everything else". It's the most visual and gives the fastest read of impact, but it needs width; under about 900px the card would stack on top.
